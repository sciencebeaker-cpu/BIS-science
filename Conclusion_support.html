Criterion C: Conclusion Guide

Science Mastery

The PESEL Framework

To achieve a Level 7-8 in your conclusion, you must move beyond simple descriptions. Use this structure to build a cohesive, theoretically sound scientific argument.

P

Point / Claim

Strand i: Stating the Conclusion

Identify the core relationship between your independent and dependent variables.

Sentence Starter: "Based on the data collected, it can be concluded that as the [Independent Variable] increased, the [Dependent Variable] [increased/decreased] with a [linear/non-linear] correlation."
Level 7-8 Tip: Define the magnitude. Don't just say it increased; say it "doubled" or "increased exponentially."
E

Evidence / Data

Strand i: Interpretation of Data

Support your point with processed data. Don't just describe the graph—quote the numbers.

Sentence Starter: "This is evidenced by the data, where at [Value 1] the rate was [Measurement 1], but at [Value 2] the result rose significantly to [Measurement 2]."
Checklist: Always include units (s, C, mm, etc.) and refer to calculated averages or means.
S

Scientific Explanation

Strand ii: Scientific Reasoning

Explain the 'Why' using the molecular-level theories we studied (Collision Theory or Le Chatelier).

Sentence Starter: "This occurs because increasing temperature provides particles with greater kinetic energy. According to Collision Theory..."

E

Evaluation of Hypothesis

Strand iii: Validating Predictions

Compare your findings to your prediction. Was the hypothesis supported, or refuted?

Sentence Starter: "These results fully support the initial hypothesis, which predicted that higher thermal energy would accelerate the decomposition of..."
L

Link / Conclusion

Strand iii: Conclusion Validity

Connect back to the original research question or real-world industrial context.

Sentence Starter: "Therefore, the relationship between thermal energy and kinetics is confirmed, providing a valid answer to the research question regarding..."
